## Scores

- **Requirements coverage — 6/10.** Sections 4–15 are an unusually detailed treatment of AC-4 dynamic authorization, role/tenant checks, passive tooling, budgets, coverage, and runtime incidents, but the declared scope in §2 excludes several P2-mandated domains: SSH/provider credential lifecycles, static-analyzer isolation, repository-to-provider prompt injection, local-source intake, optional hosted-tool enforcement, general evidence/rendering/package safety, audit-event schemas, and finding severity/confidence/validation rules.
- **Assets, attacker model, and trust boundaries — 7/10.** Sections 3.1–3.2 correctly identify the host, provider/SSH/sandbox secrets, source, target plans, evidence, privacy data, hostile repository/runtime content, compromised browser/ZAP/worker/provider processes, and operator mistakes. The model is not carried through to controls for the static-analyzer, acquisition, provider, local UI/session, evidence-preview, and packager boundaries, and it contradicts the later reliance on an in-probe interceptor as a trusted final enforcement point.
- **Safe defaults, prohibited actions, and nested-runtime isolation — 8/10.** Sections 5–6 provide a concrete no-override gate, adopt the frozen VM/rootless/compiled-Compose boundary, enumerate prohibited methods and actions, and require resource and network ceilings. It loses points because it delegates the actual Compose rejection set to architecture §10.2 instead of making the safety acceptance matrix self-contained, does not specify static-analyzer containment, and gives no independently enforced VM-internal ACL between compromised target, browser, ZAP, broker control, and evidence-collection surfaces.
- **Credential lifecycle, network egress, and production prohibition — 7/10.** Sections 4, 5.6–5.7, 7.1, 11, and DYN-02/05/11 strongly bind target credentials to run/role/tenant/origin/purpose, require one-use handles, isolate contexts, deny runtime egress, and test replay and leakage. However, `declaredSandboxOnly: true` is only an operator assertion and cannot implement the promise that a “sentinel live credential” is denied; the external-sandbox-dependency proxy lacks method/path/DNS/redirect/request/byte rules and audit receipt fields; the frozen X25519/HKDF/AES-GCM envelope profile is not actually reviewed; and SSH, provider-home, provider-token, optional-service, restart, cancellation, and engagement-cleanup lifecycles are absent.
- **Safe dynamic testing and authorization — 8/10.** The P0–P3 taxonomy in §6, role/tenant matrix in §7.2, hard budgets in §9, exact coverage semantics in §12, and negative fixture suite are specific enough to guide implementation. The score is capped because §5 creates an impossible gate order and because §§6/8 do not put method/origin/route enforcement outside the compromised browser/ZAP boundary; therefore the strongest safety promises cannot be mechanically enforced under the stated attacker model.
- **Prompt injection and hosted-service safety — 4/10.** Sections 1 and 3 correctly deny provider self-authorization, label repository/page prompt injection untrusted, and §8.1 says DOM text is not an instruction to the provider or broker. There is no provider context-construction rule, instruction precedence, tool/capability allowlist, approval non-delegation proof, deceptive-consent defense, malicious repository/provider fixture, or containment test showing Codex/Claude output cannot induce execution, egress, secret access, or evidence fabrication. Section 11.1 merely says provider inference and hosted scanners need separate approval, without defining or testing those flows.
- **Evidence integrity, privacy, redaction, and safe delivery — 7.5/10.** Sections 7.1, 8, 10, and 11 give strong dynamic-specific data minimization, lineage, screenshot re-encoding, body exclusion, secret canaries, quarantine, and package scanning. They do not specify the full evidence admission/rendering/package boundary required by P2: active-content handling, derivation integrity, safe previews, manifest/checksum/ZIP validation, post-delivery leak response, retention/deletion behavior, and package-encryption secret handling are mostly inherited or omitted rather than reviewed.
- **Safe failure, incident response, auditability, and finding governance — 6/10.** Sections 9 and 13 define useful stop triggers, no-retry behavior, credential revocation, VM destruction, cleanup review, operator notice, and new-attempt requirements. But `INTERRUPTED_BY_SAFETY` in §13.2 is not a legal frozen `PhaseAttempt.outcome`, stop sequencing does not cut externally enforced network access first or set broker/host deadlines, and there is no schema-versioned audit-event catalogue, event integrity/retention contract, severity calculation, confidence rules, validation transitions, CVSS sufficiency, dispute handling, or deterministic release calculation for Critical/High findings.
- **Control enforceability, verification, and architecture fit — 6/10.** DYN-01–16 are excellent executable fixture descriptions with packet recorders, canaries, provenance failures, containment inspection, and four-platform evidence. Yet §4 introduces canonical authorization/credential records after P1 supposedly froze contracts, without reconciling them to `Approval`, `RuntimeCapability`, OpenAPI, persistence, and state transitions; §5’s temporal contradiction makes DYN-01/03 impossible to satisfy as written; DYN-04 validates a cooperative interceptor rather than an out-of-process policy boundary; and no release tests cover static-parser escape, source acquisition, provider prompt injection, local UI authorization, hosted services, general package integrity, or audit/severity schemas.
- **Operational usability and residual-risk honesty — 8/10.** The consent disclosures in §4, explicit blocked reason codes in §12, plain-language incident notice in §13, static-only continuation, and residual risks in §16 are strong and operator-oriented. Remaining weaknesses are the lack of a deterministic explanation of how non-production credentials/data are proven rather than merely declared, no bounded incident-stop acknowledgement/deadline/escalation UX, and no operational workflow for the mandatory independent review of “every Critical/High dynamic finding and all safety-boundary evidence.”

## Overall

**6.8/10 (equal-weight average).** This is the strongest dynamic-assessment control specification of the reviewed set, but it is not a complete P2 safety specification and two contradictions in its core enforcement path prevent it from being frozen as implementation-ready.

## Strongest objection

**Must-fix:** The spec explicitly treats a compromised browser process and ZAP process as adversaries in §3.2, but §6 declares “the request interceptor is the final enforcement point,” and §§8.1–8.2 place interception/proxy behavior in the Playwright/ZAP probe path. A compromised probe can bypass its own interceptor and directly use the VM’s internal network; the spec defines no separate trusted egress proxy, per-workload firewall identity, broker-mediated request capability, or ACL proving that the browser/ZAP can reach only signed `{origin, method, path}` tuples. DYN-04 exercises prohibited behavior through the cooperative adapter, and DYN-11 proves broad external denial, but neither attempts process-level bypass or arbitrary internal requests. Consequently, the product could pass every stated dynamic release gate while a compromised browser sends a prohibited POST, reads an undeclared internal service, attacks the broker/probe control plane, or leaks session material to an approved dependency proxy. This defeats the central “cannot be bypassed through … browser” claim in §15.1.

## Other findings

1. **Must-fix — the runtime gate is temporally impossible (§§4–5).** Section 5 says all gate checks run “before any target image pull, build, container creation,” yet check 6 requires “build acquisition is finished and disconnected.” The authorization required by checks 4–7 is bound in §4 to internal origins and invalidated by a changed image digest/service map, but architecture produces image digests during `vm.build` and internal origins during `vm.start`; the proposed authorization schema does not even contain the image digest whose change must invalidate it. No implementation can satisfy this ordering without silently splitting or weakening the gate.
2. **Must-fix — the contender is a dynamic sub-spec, not the required full-product safety spec (§2).** P2 requires every trust boundary and prohibited action to have controls and tests. The scope list covers runtime only, leaving hostile static scanners/parsers, acquisition/SSH, provider compartments, local source paths, authenticated local API/UI, evidence preview, packaging, retention/deletion, and optional hosted services to architecture or later implementation.
3. **Must-fix — production-credential prevention is assertion-based (§§4–5, DYN-02).** `TargetCredentialProfile.declaredSandboxOnly: true` and operator consent do not let the engine distinguish a reused live password from a sandbox password, even though operator reuse is an explicit threat in §3.2. DYN-02 claims that a “sentinel live credential” is denied before contact but defines neither a sentinel registry/provenance signal nor an enforcement rule. The test is only achievable with fixture knowledge not represented in the product contract.
4. **Must-fix — provider prompt injection is named but not controlled (§§1, 3.2, 8.1).** Saying provider output has no policy authority and DOM text is not an instruction does not specify which repository bytes providers see, what instruction hierarchy applies, which tools are available, how proposals are quarantined, or how fake approval text is rejected. There are no Codex/Claude malicious-prompt fixtures despite architecture §17 and P7 explicitly requiring them.
5. **Must-fix — contract additions conflict with the frozen-contract premise (§4).** `RuntimeTestAuthorization` and `TargetCredentialProfile` are declared canonical extensions, but their relationship to architecture `Approval`, `CapabilityResult`, `RuntimeCapability`, `SecretHandle`, phase input digests, OpenAPI operations, database tables, revision semantics, and SSE events is unspecified. `approvedDependencyDestinations: string[]` is materially weaker than frozen structured approval destinations, and `purpose: "target-login"|"target-service"` does not cleanly reconcile with architecture’s `"probe"|"target-service"` secret purposes.
6. **Must-fix — incident state and stop mechanics are not implementable as written (§13.2).** `INTERRUPTED_BY_SAFETY` is not a legal architecture `PhaseAttempt.outcome`; there is only `INTERRUPTED`. The sequence closes browser contexts before removing network access, which is unsafe against the compromised-process threat, and supplies no stop deadline, heartbeat threshold, escalation timer, host-level network-cut primitive, or crash/restart reconciliation rule specific to an incident.
7. **Must-fix — audit events are prose, not a contract (§§10–14).** Request-occurrence fields and “canonical audit” locations are useful, but there is no enumerated event schema for authorization create/revoke/expire, credential-handle create/redeem/reject/revoke, plan invalidation, probe request allow/deny, egress decision/attempt, resource-limit trip, incident stop/escalation, VM cleanup residue, review, or release gate. DYN-15’s “audit … complete” therefore has no objective oracle.
8. **Must-fix — finding governance is absent (§§10, 13, 15).** The spec uses low confidence, Critical/High, independent review, and release blocking without defining technical-severity inputs, business-priority separation, confidence thresholds, validation-state transitions, CVSS eligibility, imported/tool finding treatment, reviewer conflicts, or how disputed/invalidated findings affect release. P2 explicitly requires these rules.
9. **Must-fix — the external sandbox dependency exception is under-specified (§§4–5, 11, DYN-11).** The schema stores destination strings but no methods, paths, redirects, DNS resolution pin, data categories, credential binding, request/byte ceilings, revocation propagation, or egress receipt. DYN-11 tests denial of broad egress but not the approved exception as an exfiltration channel or a compromised probe’s attempt to reuse it.
10. **Should-fix — the secret-envelope review is incomplete (§§5, 7, 11, DYN-05).** Replay, wrong-recipient, expiry, and tmpfs cleanup are tested, but the safety spec never explicitly adopts or challenges architecture §10.3’s X25519/HKDF/AES-GCM profile, associated-data fields, key authenticity, nonce rules, server/helper restart behavior, cancellation before/after redemption, or failed-destroy cleanup.
11. **Should-fix — crawler and signed-plan semantics need a finite rule (§§6, 8.2).** P1 allows statically discovered or operator-seeded routes while ZAP may spider and the interceptor admits only requests “explicitly represented” in the signed plan. State whether a signed route-template rule can admit newly discovered concrete URLs, define normalization and query handling, and test encoding/canonicalization bypasses; otherwise crawl behavior is either nonfunctional or broader than the consented route set.
12. **Should-fix — mandatory review is operationally undefined (§15.1).** “A separate security reviewer validates every Critical/High dynamic finding and all safety-boundary evidence” lacks reviewer identity/independence, required questions, evidence access, pass/fail rubric, objection handling, timeout/unavailable state, and delta invalidation when evidence or policy changes.

## Concrete fixes

1. Move method/origin/path enforcement out of Playwright/ZAP into a release-owned, separately isolated proxy or broker capability that the probes cannot bypass. Give each workload a distinct network identity; deny target/probe access to broker control, helper relay, admission, other runs, and undeclared services. Add process-compromise fixtures that use raw sockets, alternate protocols, direct IPs, encoded paths, DNS rebinding, proxy bypass, and session-cookie theft.
2. Split §5 into an explicit staged state machine: preflight/compile gate; operator approval for build acquisition; isolated acquisition; network removal and build; post-build image/service/origin attestation; control-plan generation; final dynamic authorization; credential redemption; probe. Freeze which digest each stage consumes, what invalidates it, and the exact blocked outcome. Never require a later-stage fact before the stage that produces it.
3. Reconcile the new safety contracts with P1 rather than saying DTOs “MUST be extended.” Provide versioned schemas, persistence keys, OpenAPI mutations, SSE/audit events, legal revision/state transitions, digest inputs, and mapping to `Approval`, `RuntimeCapability`, `SecretHandle`, broker operations, and coverage.
4. Replace bare `declaredSandboxOnly` assurance with a precise responsibility and evidence model: operator attestation and warning when the product cannot verify provenance, deterministic rejection of known production endpoint/credential canaries, optional customer-supplied sandbox issuer/audience/account constraints, and an honest blocked state when non-production status cannot be established. Narrow DYN-02 to claims the engine can actually prove.
5. Add the omitted full-P2 control matrices for acquisition/local paths and SSH, provider credentials/homes and prompt injection, static analyzer containers, optional hosted tools, local API/UI session, evidence display/admission, report/package integrity, retention/deletion, and post-delivery incidents. Each row needs asset, adversary, allowed action, deny rule, enforcement component, audit event, failure state, owner, and negative fixture.
6. Define provider prompt-injection controls for both launch paths: release-owned instruction precedence, redacted evidence views, exact provider command allowlist, no helper/broker/secret/approval/package authority, model output as quarantined proposal, approval UI provenance, and malicious fixtures for shell/tool requests, egress, secrets, policy override, evidence fabrication, and deceptive consent.
7. Define the approved dependency proxy contract using structured destinations plus method/path/data/credential/request/byte constraints, DNS/redirect behavior, expiry/revocation, policy digest, and egress receipts. Test both legitimate access and target/probe exfiltration attempts through that allowed channel.
8. Make emergency stop an externally enforced, deadline-bound sequence: immediately cut probe/target/dependency network identities; fence and revoke secrets/approvals; stop admission; issue bounded broker stop/destroy; escalate to host `vm.emergencyStop`; reconcile journal/residue after helper/server restart; and map the result to legal frozen attempt/run/phase states with a safety reason code.
9. Add a schema-versioned audit-event catalogue with trigger, actor/workload, run/attempt/fence, decision and reason, policy/approval digests, safe metadata, integrity ordering, retention, and package/export rules. Use those schemas as the DYN-15 oracle.
10. Freeze finding governance: technical severity, business priority, confidence, validation status, CVSS sufficiency, imported finding behavior, independent-review transitions, dispute/supersession, and deterministic release-gate calculation.
11. Explicitly review the architecture VM-envelope cryptographic profile and add issue/redeem/cancel/crash/restart/destroy timing tests, key/recipient/approval/purpose AAD checks, nonce uniqueness, replay cache retention, zeroization, and residue behavior.
12. Define route-template canonicalization and crawler expansion: normalized path/query rules, finite same-origin discovery, authorization digest impact, encoded traversal/case/Unicode handling, and a hard rule that newly observed content can propose but cannot enlarge the active plan without reauthorization.

## Verdict

**Revise.** Contender 4 clears the bar for a high-quality safe-dynamic-testing design: its safety classes, role/tenant matrix, budgets, coverage semantics, and adversarial fixture catalogue are substantive and worth grafting into the final spec. It misses the P2 ship bar because the full-product trust boundary is incomplete, the gate cannot execute in its stated order, and the central request policy can be bypassed by an adversary the spec explicitly models.
