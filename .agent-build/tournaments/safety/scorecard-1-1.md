## Scores

- **Requirements coverage — 8.0/10.** The contender covers nearly every P2-required area with unusually concrete controls, including hostile-source acquisition (§5), nested runtime (§§8–10), credentials (§§4, 8.3), egress (§10), prompt injection (§7), redaction (§12), emergency stop (§13), release tests (§14), and residual risks (§16); however, P2's explicit runtime-test safety classes, finding severity/confidence/validation rules, audit-event contract, and residual-risk acceptance workflow are missing or incomplete.
- **Assets, adversaries, and trust boundaries — 9.5/10.** Sections 2–4 identify people, source, credentials, evidence, package integrity, other runs, malicious repositories/endpoints, compromised parsers/browsers/daemons/providers, and the trusted computing base, then map most of them to compartment and data-lifecycle requirements. The main omission is that the permitted worker-output channel is not itself modeled as an exfiltration boundary after a target receives a sandbox secret.
- **Attack-path completeness — 8.0/10.** The principal escape, egress, prompt-injection, confused-deputy, replay, stale-fence, resource-exhaustion, active-content, and evidence-forgery chains are traced and fixture-backed, but the chain `sandbox secret/source -> malicious target -> transformed or encrypted log/screenshot/artifact -> declared receipt -> quarantine/admission -> customer ZIP` survives SI-03 and §12's exact secret scans. SSH-agent signing-oracle/key-enumeration exposure is also not closed.
- **Hostile-source and nested-runtime isolation — 9.5/10.** Sections 5, 6, 8, and 9 specify bounded no-follow acquisition, inert baseline analyzers, a mount-free disposable VM, rootless Docker, a typed broker, strict Compose regeneration, resource ceilings, and preflight/drift attestation, with an extensive negative corpus in §14. The residual hypervisor/kernel and trusted-parser risks are correctly disclosed rather than hidden.
- **Credential lifecycle and authentication isolation — 8.5/10.** Provider homes, exact acquisition inputs, one-use purpose/recipient-bound X25519 envelopes, tmpfs delivery, expiry/replay rejection, revocation, cancellation cleanup, and package exclusions are strong. The score is reduced because target-controlled artifacts can launder a deliberately supplied sandbox credential and because the allowed SSH-agent path lacks a fingerprint-filtering/signing proxy or equivalent proof that unrelated agent keys cannot be enumerated or used.
- **Network egress, consent, and production prohibition — 9.0/10.** Section 10 separates build and runtime identities, combines a root-owned firewall with an authenticated proxy, handles redirects/rebinding/IPv6/metadata/LAN/bypass paths, and explicitly concedes that an approved registry remains an exfiltration recipient. Runtime is external-network denied and production-like integrations are blocked; the remaining limitation is the architecture-approved build-egress channel, for which recipient scoping cannot constrain malicious payload semantics.
- **Prompt injection and external-service safety — 9.5/10.** Section 7 makes target text non-authoritative, constrains providers to five ID-based operations over an allowlisted evidence view, removes helper/runtime/approval/package authority, blocks repository-owned instructions/plugins/MCP, and verifies both providers with adversarial fixtures. It also candidly records the provider-process credential residual and treats optional destinations as explicit, separate approvals.
- **Evidence integrity, privacy, logs, screenshots, and packaging — 8.0/10.** Sections 6 and 12 provide fenced closed receipts, quarantine, hardened normalization, typed report ASTs, attachment-only unsafe media, metadata-stripped image derivation, allowlisted logs, repeated scanning, immutable staging, and ZIP reopen validation. Those controls detect known/plain secret forms but do not prevent a malicious secret-bearing target from exporting transformed, split, encrypted, or steganographic secret material through a declared artifact or screenshot.
- **Safe failure, incident response, auditability, and finding governance — 7.0/10.** Sections 11 and 13 give strong fail-closed resource behavior, trusted-host emergency stop, fencing, credential rotation guidance, exact cleanup, and no automatic resume. However, `SECURITY_HOLD` in §13.2 is absent from architecture §6.1's frozen run states; the spec does not define a typed audit-event catalog or finding classification/validation rules; and §15's “unaccepted” Critical/High condition has no residual-risk acceptance actor, record, expiry, or revocation semantics.
- **Verification, enforceability, and architecture fit — 8.0/10.** Section 14 is an excellent adversarial suite covering acquisition, analyzers, providers, Compose, runtime escape, egress, resources, secrets, protocol replay, active content, redaction, restart, and honest blocked coverage. Buildability is weakened by the new non-canonical incident state, missing dynamic-test authorization contract, missing audit/finding policy, and the absence of fixtures for transformed-secret artifact laundering and SSH-agent key isolation.

## Overall

**8.5/10.** Equal-weight average. This is a strong, implementation-oriented hostile-repository safety specification and clears the quality threshold, but it is not ship-ready until the permitted artifact channel cannot launder runtime secrets and the safety lifecycle is reconciled with the frozen contracts.

## Strongest objection

The contender's one-way data-flow invariant does not close the most important remaining exfiltration path. SI-03 permits “worker output” to move as declared receipts into quarantine; §8.3 deliberately delivers a sandbox credential to a named target service or probe; §12 permits logs, screenshots, traces, and declared artifacts to progress after redaction; and §12's scanner looks for seeded values, credential forms, and known sensitive material. A malicious target that knows the supplied secret can split, encrypt, hash-with-a-target-known key, encode, or steganographically render it into a log, downloadable artifact, HTTP response, or screenshot. Exact/known-form scanning no longer recognizes the value, yet the declared receipt is structurally valid and can be admitted and packaged. Network denial and lack of direct package access do not prevent this indirect channel. This violates the brief's AC-5 seeded-secret guarantee and the contender's own claim in §1(6) that a compromised target cannot expose credentials through package staging. The safety spec must restrict what can cross from a secret-bearing runtime to trusted-probe-generated structured results or explicitly quarantine all target-controlled output from customer export; redaction alone cannot establish noninterference.

## Other findings

1. **Must-fix — `SECURITY_HOLD` contradicts the frozen lifecycle.** Section 13.2 requires the host monitor to “atomically mark the run `SECURITY_HOLD`,” and §15.2 blocks packages on an active hold. Architecture §6.1 defines an exhaustive `RunState` enum and legal transitions with no `SECURITY_HOLD`; the DB, OpenAPI, SSE, reducers, recovery, and package eligibility therefore have no implementable shared contract. Either version the canonical state model or map the incident to an existing state plus a separately typed security disposition.
2. **Must-fix — runtime-test safety classes are not defined.** Section 10.2 distinguishes a “Playwright/passive probe” and denies mutating HTTP methods unless separately approved, but it never defines the P2-required classes for passive observation, unauthenticated read, authenticated read, reversible sandbox mutation, or prohibited destructive/external action. There is no per-control contract binding class to origin, routes/methods, request data, credential recipient, rate/side-effect budget, cleanup, and coverage result. A login POST and a destructive POST are both merely “mutating” at the point the engine needs deterministic authorization.
3. **Must-fix — finding governance is absent.** The architecture's `Finding` has technical severity, business priority, confidence, validation state, CWE, and CVSS fields, while P2 requires rules for them. The contender only uses Critical/High labels for release blockers and asks an independent reviewer to approve components (§15.1). It does not define severity derivation, confidence evidence, imported-finding treatment, corroboration versus reproduction, disputes/invalidations, supersession, or how unresolved states affect coverage and package release.
4. **Must-fix — auditability is asserted but not contracted.** SI-01–SI-10 are said to be “continuously attested,” and §§8, 10, 12, and 13 name several records, yet no enumerated event vocabulary defines trusted producer, emission point, safe fields, run/attempt/fence/approval correlation, integrity ordering, retention, or export status for approvals, secret issue/redeem/revoke, provider dispatch, egress decisions, policy denials, attestation drift, evidence admission, security stop, cleanup residue, and package release. Architecture §8.8's generic events do not carry enough security-specific facts without a frozen extension.
5. **Must-fix — the SSH-agent path does not isolate a selected key.** The data map permits “SSH key/agent” input in the acquisition worker, and §5.1 disables agent forwarding but does not define an agent-filtering proxy, allowed public-key fingerprint, confirmation requirement, maximum signing requests, or proof that other keys exposed by a host agent cannot be enumerated. A malicious Git/SSH endpoint is explicitly in the threat model, so an exact socket mount is not equivalent to an exact credential.
6. **Must-fix — residual-risk acceptance has no authority or lifecycle.** Section 15.1 blocks only “unaccepted” Critical/High vulnerabilities, and §16 lists serious residuals, but the document never says who may accept which risk, what evidence and rationale are required, whether acceptance is product-, installation-, engagement-, or run-scoped, when it expires, what cannot be accepted, or what change revokes it. P2 explicitly requires residual-risk acceptance; prose disclosure alone is not acceptance governance.
7. **Should-fix — some security-stop triggers lack an observable detector.** Section 13.1 triggers on a provider credential “read/transmission attempt,” unexpected host effects, and VM/hypervisor escape indicators, but does not identify the trusted sensor or audit evidence for every trigger. In particular, a provider process already possesses its own credential while sending TLS traffic to an allowed endpoint; distinguishing authorized authentication from model-induced credential disclosure may be impossible at the network boundary. Separate directly detectable triggers from heuristic indicators and define their containment threshold.
8. **Should-fix — compromised daemon integrity is under-disclosed.** Sections 2.2 and 14.3 assume a compromised rootless daemon and test that it cannot read physical-host/provider canaries, which is good blast-radius validation. They do not address that a daemon can lie to the trusted broker about container state, logs, image identity, or runtime outcomes, degrading dynamic-evidence integrity even without escape. The result should be bounded to untrusted/uncorroborated dynamic evidence or explicitly disclosed as a residual limitation.

## Concrete fixes

1. Define a provenance-based runtime export policy. After any target service receives a secret, prohibit target-controlled arbitrary files/logs/downloads from customer export; admit only fixed-schema results and screenshots generated by a trusted probe under a predefined capture plan, and treat all target bytes as secret-suspected. Add split/base-N, compression, encryption, chunking across artifacts, image/QR/steganographic rendering, and cross-artifact reconstruction fixtures. If screenshots or arbitrary outputs must remain, explicitly state that AC-5 cannot be proven and require human risk acceptance.
2. Reconcile incident state with `rak-contract/1.0.0`: either add a versioned `SECURITY_HOLD` state and every legal transition across DTOs, migrations, API/SSE, reducer, recovery, revision, deletion, and package gates, or retain the canonical run state and add a typed, durable blocking `SecurityDisposition` referenced by capability, dispatch, admission, review, and packaging checks.
3. Add a versioned runtime-control authorization schema with safety class, exact origin, methods/routes, request-data category, credential purpose/recipient, maximum requests/rate, allowed side effect, rollback/cleanup evidence, abort triggers, and result-on-denial. Include redirect, JavaScript fetch, WebSocket, upload/download, login, reversible mutation, email/payment/webhook, and production-sentinel fixtures.
4. Publish normative security-finding rules: separate technical severity, business priority, confidence, and validation; define CVSS/CWE use, evidence thresholds, imported scanner results, reviewer independence, reproduction/corroboration, dispute/invalidity, supersession, and release effects for every state.
5. Add a schema-versioned security audit catalog with event type, trusted producer, trigger, required correlation IDs/digests, safe payload allowlist, transactional ordering, retention, and package/export treatment. Test missing, duplicated, reordered, stale-fence, and forged events.
6. Put an allowlisting proxy in front of any host SSH agent. Bind one approved public-key fingerprint and Git destination, deny identity enumeration beyond that key, cap and audit signing requests without recording signatures, revoke the proxy after acquisition, and test a malicious SSH server requesting unrelated keys/signatures. Otherwise remove agent-socket support and accept exact key files only.
7. Define residual-risk decisions with authorized role, scope, affected control/assets, evidence, rationale, compensating controls, expiry, review trigger, revocation, and non-acceptable categories. Host-socket access, production access, secret/package leakage, runtime egress bypass, and integrity-gate bypass must remain non-waivable.
8. Add an enforcement/observation matrix for every §13 trigger and distinguish deterministic security holds from warning/triage signals. Document dynamic-evidence trust degradation after daemon/probe compromise and require independent corroboration before such evidence supports a material finding or recommendation.

## Verdict

**revise.** The contender clears the 8/10 tournament bar and contributes the strongest hostile-source, nested-runtime, prompt-injection, and release-test material expected from P2. It misses the ship bar because a secret-bearing hostile target can still use the permitted artifact path as an encoded exfiltration channel, and several required safety contracts—incident state, runtime action classes, audit events, finding governance, and residual-risk acceptance—remain undefined or incompatible with the frozen architecture.
