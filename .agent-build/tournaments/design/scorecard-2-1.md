# Design Contender 2 — Scorecard

## Scores

- **Lay comprehension — 9.0/10.** The three truth levels in §1, consequence-first finding structure in §4.7, seven-criterion decision comparison in §4.9, and sentence pattern in §12.2 give a non-technical owner a clear route from impact to uncertainty to proof; the score is capped because the mandatory review experience still assumes unexplained access and role mechanics.
- **Intake completeness without interrogation — 7.3/10.** Section 4.2 captures all ten frozen discovery topics, provenance, unknowns, source choice, profiles, credentials, and target identity, but requiring an operator who does not know an answer to also supply “reason, confidence effect, coverage effect, and follow-up owner” turns uncertainty into a four-part assessment exercise rather than a forgiving interview.
- **Safety clarity — 8.8/10.** “Show the boundary before the button,” exact optional-service disclosures, one-use secret handles, immutable-target review, and attachment-only evidence previews are concrete and consistent with the architecture; first-run provider authentication and denied-auth recovery are conspicuously absent.
- **Capability-gate and blocked-state honesty — 9.3/10.** Sections 4.5–4.6 explicitly separate capable, blocked, not applicable, and not tested states, retain attempted safe steps and coverage effects, and continue static work without suggesting a policy-relaxation shortcut.
- **Progress/resume usability — 8.5/10.** The 14-phase path, SSE disconnect/replay copy, fenced-attempt explanation, scoped retries, pause/cancel cleanup states, and durable residue limitation are strong; recovery after failed reviews is asserted but not designed.
- **Evidence traceability — 9.2/10.** The trace rail, evidence catalog/detail, separate occurrence and digest, provenance activity, redaction state, safe preview, and finding/decision links closely follow the frozen evidence DTOs and keep proof one step away.
- **Report readability — 9.1/10.** Sections 13.1–13.8 define a coherent package index and five report types with reading order, plain-language exclusions, print rules, provenance references, and accessible static HTML/Markdown; actual lay-review handoff before package release remains unresolved.
- **Accessibility — 8.8/10.** Semantic table/card equivalents, focus behavior, live-region limits, WCAG 2.2 AA targets, 200%/400% reflow, forced colors, keyboard journeys, and screen-reader coverage are implementation-grade, though the quick-inspector sheet versus route behavior needs a single focus/history contract.
- **Responsive local use — 6.8/10.** The breakpoint matrix and 320px adaptations are detailed, but §10.2 requires a `220px stepper + 720px form + 280px summary` at `xl ≥1280` inside a shell that §6.1 gives a 240px rail and `clamp(16px, 2.2vw, 32px)` main padding; at 1280px only about 984px remains before gaps for a 1220px composition, guaranteeing overflow or an undocumented reflow.
- **Implementation specificity — 7.1/10.** Components, tokens, routes, canonical states, OpenAPI discipline, copy, and QA fixtures are unusually specific, but §4.10’s failed-review correction flow and reviewer handoff are not implementable from the frozen route/API/state model, while provider-login and authorization-denied states remain generic.

## Overall

**8.4/10.** The contender clears the tournament’s numerical quality bar and is strongest where this product most needs integrity: evidence truth, limitation visibility, decision fairness, and report readability. It does not yet clear the implementation-ready bar because a release-blocking human review can neither be handed to the intended reviewer nor corrected through a defined lifecycle, and one normative responsive composition cannot fit its declared breakpoint.

## Strongest objection

**Must-fix: the mandatory human-review flow has no credible reviewer handoff or correction lifecycle.** Section 4.10 requires technical and lay reviews and says “A failed review returns the run to the appropriate correction flow,” while §4.11 blocks packaging until those gates pass. Yet the product is defined in §3.1 as “local and single-operator,” the route inventory has no reviewer/read-only route or handoff mode, and the design never says how a customer software owner or separate technical reviewer receives the material, opens the loopback-only UI, establishes identity, or submits a review without gaining operator powers. The frozen architecture exposes `createReview` only in `REVIEW_REQUIRED`; findings, decision output, and admitted evidence are immutable, and no correction mutation exists. As written, teams must either invent a cross-user access model, let the operator self-attest as every reviewer, or dead-end after a failed gate. That undermines the brief’s customer usability test, the architecture’s independent/human review guarantee, and the package release path.

## Other findings

1. **Must-fix — the desktop setup layout cannot fit its own breakpoint.** Section 10.2 assigns 1220px of fixed columns before gaps at `xl ≥1280`; §§6.1 and 10.2 simultaneously consume 240px for the run rail and roughly 56px for main padding. The resulting content width is about 984px at the breakpoint. This directly violates §10.1’s no-page-overflow guarantee and forces P6 to invent a different breakpoint or composition.
2. **Must-fix — first-run provider authentication guidance is missing.** Section 4.1 covers the one-time UI bootstrap token, not Codex/Claude authentication. “System readiness” is only inventoried in §§3.1 and 5; there is no login/status flow, unauthenticated/expired state, launcher command, environment-credential alternative, provider-specific remediation, or return path. P3 explicitly requires first-run authentication guidance, and the architecture freezes launcher `login` and `status` verbs.
3. **Must-fix — failed-review correction is incompatible with the frozen contract.** The sentence in §4.10 promises an “appropriate correction flow,” but §3.3 has no correction/revision route from review, §5 has no correction screen, and §15.3 says the web consumes only the frozen OpenAPI client. The spec must identify whether failure creates a child run revision, reruns synthesis, supersedes reports/reviews, or can be resolved by a new review, and it must use only legal architecture transitions.
4. **Should-fix — marking a discovery topic unknown is too demanding for the intended user.** Section 4.2 and `UnknownField` in §7.1 require four freeform judgments precisely when the user lacks knowledge. This risks fabricated boilerplate and intake abandonment. The design should let the operator state why it is unknown and who may answer, while the system proposes plain-language confidence/coverage effects for confirmation.
5. **Should-fix — authorization-denied states are not specified with the promised completeness.** The universal matrix in §7.3 covers disabled, errors, and offline data, and §15.3 lists HTTP 403, but there is no concrete treatment for denied optional-service approval, denied runtime egress, revoked secret capability, expired approval, or provider permission failure beyond local component prose. P3 requires every denied state; these materially affect coverage and recovery.
6. **Should-fix — provider selection can outrun provider readiness.** Setup step 1 lets the operator select a provider, while bootstrap derives a launcher provider and System readiness is not tied to the selection. The design does not say whether the other provider is selectable, how unavailable authentication is indicated, or whether switching providers requires a new launcher/engagement. This can lead to a fully completed six-step intake that cannot start.
7. **Should-fix — the evidence quick inspector and route model have ambiguous history/focus behavior.** Section 3.3 mandates route-based evidence details, while `EvidenceInspector` in §7.1 opens a 420px sheet from lists and a full-screen dialog on small screens. Specify whether opening updates the URL, how browser Back behaves, and where focus returns; otherwise deep links, scroll restoration, and accessible dismissal will diverge.
8. **Nice-to-have — the executive report’s 4–7 printed-page target lacks an overflow policy.** Section 13.2 defines a strong reading order but not what moves to appendices when three principal issues, option comparisons, unknowns, and long owner context exceed the target. A deterministic prioritization/truncation rule would preserve report consistency without hiding material caveats.

## Concrete fixes

1. Define a reviewer journey that fits the local security model: who can review, how the operator hands over the workstation or creates a bounded read-only review session, what identity/attestation is recorded, which material is frozen, and which actions are unavailable. If independent reviewer authentication is intentionally out of scope, label human reviews as local attestations and require an externally signed artifact for stronger claims.
2. Map failed review to one exact architecture-valid state sequence. Specify whether the UI creates a child revision, which inputs are copied, which phase reruns, how old objections/reviews remain visible, and when a replacement review can unblock packaging. Add the necessary route only if it is backed by the frozen API; otherwise flag the architecture contract for revision rather than inventing frontend behavior.
3. Recalculate responsive widths against the shell’s actual inner inline size. Move the three-column setup composition to a container-query threshold of at least its columns plus gaps and padding, collapse the summary inline earlier, and add a 1280px-with-240px-rail fixture to the QA matrix.
4. Add a first-run authentication state machine to System readiness: authenticated, unauthenticated, expired, provider unavailable, and unsupported launcher/provider combination. Give exact `start-codex.sh`/`start-cc.sh login|status` guidance, safe credential alternatives, remediation copy, and return-to-setup behavior.
5. Replace four blank unknown-impact fields with constrained, plain-language defaults derived from the topic and reason, editable before confirmation. Retain the canonical strings in the submitted DTO without asking operators to perform confidence modeling unaided.
6. Add explicit screen/component treatments for denied, expired, and revoked capabilities, including effect on planned controls, safe next action, and when denial is final versus revisitable.
7. Freeze the evidence-inspector navigation contract: URL behavior, breakpoint transition, focus destination/restoration, browser Back semantics, safe-preview loading/error states, and preservation of list filters/scroll.
8. Add executive-report overflow rules that rank decision-critical content, move technical detail—not limitations—to linked appendices, and fail the plain-language gate if required material cannot fit coherently.

## Verdict

**Revise.** The contender clears the 8/10 tournament score bar and should remain a leading synthesis candidate, but it misses the “implementation-ready from the frozen contracts” bar. Repair the reviewer handoff/correction lifecycle, provider-authentication flow, and impossible 1280px setup composition before adopting it as the final design spec.
