## Scores

- **Lay-user fit — 9/10.** The “careful senior consultant” thesis is carried into concrete plain-language patterns such as “Runtime checks can’t run safely yet,” explicit unknowns, business-consequence-first findings, and a lay review gate; however, setup still exposes terms such as “X25519 recipient,” “source handle,” “snapshot digest,” and “capability state” without specifying the nearby plain-language translation on those exact screens.
- **Complete guided flow and off-path states — 8/10.** Sections 4.1–4.8 cover expired bootstrap links, missing prerequisites, dirty trees, acquisition errors, unknown/conflicting discovery, blocked runtime, waiting input, retry, pause, cancel, reconnect, failed validation, packaging, and recoverable deletion unusually well. The correction path after a failed human review is only “Return to affected section” plus “the architecture’s new input/revision behavior” (§4.6), with no defined correction object, affected-section behavior, supersession flow, re-review scope, or way back to packaging.
- **Visual/typographic craft — 9/10.** The “paper, ink, and evidence thread” direction is distinctive but restrained, and §§7–8 provide implementable color, spacing, elevation, motion, font, fluid scale, line-height, measure, and vertical-rhythm systems rather than a mood board. The few remaining issues are implementation-level, chiefly the vague “60% contrast” disabled treatment in `ChoiceCard`, which is not a testable token and could undercut the otherwise exact contrast system.
- **Evidence/limitation clarity — 9/10.** Coverage avoids a misleading score, six states are separately defined, evidence previews are metadata-first and bounded, findings separate severity/priority/confidence/validation, and decision cells expose unverified/conflicting states. It loses a point because the product-claim provenance input cannot yet express the complete evidence taxonomy required by the frozen contract.
- **Safety consent — 9/10.** The safety review orders exposure, denies wildcard egress, makes hosted services separate opt-ins, hides secret values, supports denial and static continuation, and requires a clear authorization/production-safety attestation before resolution. The provider and optional-service choices appear both in Engagement and Safety without a specified review/confirmation relationship, leaving a small risk that an early selection looks more final than the later approval.
- **Accessibility — 9/10.** §10 specifies keyboard paths, focus movement, semantics, announcement throttling, target sizes, 400% reflow, text-spacing overrides, reduced motion/contrast handling, safe-preview access, and a credible manual/automated verification matrix. The disabled-card “60% contrast” rule and the select-style section navigation need exact semantic/contrast acceptance criteria before this reaches 10.
- **Responsive behavior — 9/10.** The spec names a 320 px floor, breakpoints, container queries, gutters, widths, safe areas, keyboard behavior, long-token wrapping, table/card transformations, and per-screen reflow at four width bands. It is stronger than an assertion of responsiveness, though several dense views still rely on prose rather than a representative narrow wireframe proving hierarchy at 320 px.
- **Component specificity — 8/10.** The component catalog and universal state contract are detailed enough to drive most frontend work, including focus, loading, error, stale, and reconnect behavior. `DiscoveryPrompt` is materially incomplete: §4.2 only defines “Owner told us,” “Not yet verified,” and a conflict composer, while architecture §8.2 requires all seven provenance values and conditional inputs for owner, analytics, code inference, and conflicts.
- **Implementability — 8/10.** Screen inventory, tokens, components, state rules, acceptance criteria, and architecture references make this highly buildable. Rework is still likely around the incomplete provenance mapping, the hand-waved correction/revision loop, and the unresolved relationship between the launchers selecting a provider image (architecture §5.1 / `launcherProvider`) and the UI offering two freely selectable provider radio cards (§4.2).

## Overall

**8.7/10** — unweighted average. This is a strong contender with unusually complete interaction, typography, accessibility, and responsive guidance, but it is not implementation-ready until the discovery contract and failed-review correction loop are made explicit.

## Strongest objection

**Must-fix: the primary discovery control cannot faithfully create the frozen `ProductClaim` contract.** In §4.2, `DiscoveryPrompt` says the provenance selector defaults to “Owner told us” or “Not yet verified,” and only separately specifies a conflicting-claim composer. Frozen architecture §8.2 requires `owner-stated`, `documented`, `observed`, `analytics-supported`, `code-inferred`, `unverified`, and `conflicting`, with `speakerRole`/capture time for owner claims, dataset/query/window metadata for analytics, inference reasoning for code inference, and at least two references for conflicts. This is not a cosmetic label omission: operators will either be unable to record documented/observed/analytics/code-inferred claims, or engineers will invent a hidden mapping and conditional fields. Either outcome undermines the brief’s core requirement to keep owner statements, observation, analytics, inference, and uncertainty distinct.

## Other findings

1. **Must-fix — failed-review correction is a named transition, not a designed flow (§§4.5–4.6).** “Corrections create new inputs/revision as required” and “Return to affected section” do not say what is editable, what is superseded, how immutable evidence/reviews remain visible, which validations become stale, whether both human reviews must repeat, or how the operator returns to the gate. This is the most important off-path between synthesis and a releasable package and will otherwise be designed ad hoc during implementation.
2. **Must-fix — provider selection authority is ambiguous (§4.2 Step 1 versus architecture §5.1 and `getSystem.launcherProvider`).** The design presents Codex and Claude Code as two enabled radio cards, while the architecture says each launcher selects its pinned provider image and exposes the launcher provider. The spec must say whether the alternate card is unavailable, starts a separate launcher flow, or is genuinely executable by the current server. A choice that cannot run would strand the user after substantial intake.
3. **Should-fix — draft persistence timing is internally unclear (§4.2).** The flow diagram places “Create run / save DRAFT” after confirmation, while Step 1 says autosave begins “after the run can be created,” and the architecture can create a run once source is known. Specify the exact creation boundary, reload behavior before it, and how local values survive or are intentionally discarded; otherwise a long four-chapter discovery session risks inconsistent persistence behavior.
4. **Should-fix — the validation issue ledger is under-specified (§4.7 and screen inventory).** It is the recovery surface for deterministic release blockers, but has no issue anatomy, grouping, severity/blocking distinction, link behavior, retained-data statement, stale/revalidate state, or component definition comparable to findings and recovery. “Affected section → validate again” is insufficient when one validation error may span multiple artifacts or require a new revision.
5. **Should-fix — the Runs index is a weak spot in an otherwise exhaustive flow (§5 and §9.2).** Its inventory says only “Resume drafts, active runs, completed packages,” and responsive rules cover card/list layout but not empty-first-run guidance, failed/cancelled/tombstoned runs, revision lineage, duplicate names, pagination, or what the primary action is when a run needs attention. This is the durable home and recovery entry point, so these states matter.
6. **Nice-to-have — exact setup copy should translate the remaining specialist terms.** §11.3 allows Git, SSH, ZIP, and SHA-256 with nearby Help definitions, but the package flow also foregrounds X25519 and the scope flow exposes digest/manifest/LFS vocabulary. Provide inline plain-language glosses on first encounter, not only Help, to fully meet the calm non-specialist requirement.
7. **Nice-to-have — replace “Disabled keeps 60% contrast” in `ChoiceCard` (§6.2).** A percentage opacity/contrast treatment is ambiguous and can conflict with §10’s 4.5:1 text and 3:1 boundary requirements. Name exact disabled foreground/background/border tokens and test pairings.

## Concrete fixes

1. Expand `DiscoveryPrompt` into a complete provenance state table: user-facing label, allowed topic state, required conditional fields, evidence-link behavior, validation copy, summary display, and exact `ProductClaim` mapping for all seven values. Include `capturedAt` behavior and the two-reference minimum for conflicts.
2. Add a correction/revision flow under §4.6 with explicit entry from each disputed/invalidated checklist item, editable versus immutable objects, supersession preview, affected gates/validators, saved-draft/conflict behavior, revision lineage, and the exact route back through independent/human review to packaging.
3. Resolve provider authority: bind the run to `launcherProvider` and render the other provider as guidance to relaunch, or amend the flow with an architecture-supported provider switch. Define unavailable/auth-expired provider states before the operator starts discovery.
4. Define when `createRun` occurs and which wizard steps are server-durable. Add reload/session-expiry behavior for pre-run local state and for every post-creation chapter.
5. Specify a `ValidationIssueLedger` component and Runs-index state matrix, including empty, attention, failed, cancelled, terminal, tombstoned, revision-parent, pagination, and retry/recovery entry states.
6. Replace relative disabled styling with exact accessible tokens, and add inline definitions for X25519, snapshot digest, file manifest, and LFS at the screens where actions depend on them.

## Verdict

**Revise.** The contender clears the bar for visual craft, accessibility, responsive rigor, safety framing, and evidence honesty. It misses the ship bar because its central discovery interaction does not yet implement the frozen provenance contract, and its most consequential off-path—the failed-review correction/revision loop—is not designed deeply enough to build without product decisions being made in code.
